# AGM vs EFB adoption trends

The shift from conventional SLI to EFB and AGM batteries is driven by one thing: start-stop systems and higher electrical loads in newer vehicles. Adoption is uneven across markets and tracks the vehicle parc.

!!! info "Data classification"
    This page is a **structural analysis** based on publicly available information about vehicle
    standards and battery technology. It contains **no market-size, growth-rate or share figures**.
    Any qualitative statement is marked *indicative*. For project-specific intelligence, contact
    Chengguang directly.

## The driver: start-stop and electrical load

Start-stop systems cycle the battery far more often than a conventional vehicle. A standard SLI battery is not built for that duty, which is why vehicle makers specify **EFB** (enhanced flooded) or **AGM** (absorbent glass mat).

- **EFB** improves cycling durability over SLI at a modest cost premium.
- **AGM** supports higher charge acceptance and deeper cycling, at a higher cost.

## Adoption pattern

*Indicative:*

| Market type | Typical technology mix |
|---|---|
| Older vehicle parc | SLI dominant |
| Growing parc of newer vehicles | SLI + EFB, with AGM on premium/start-stop models |
| Mature parc with widespread start-stop | EFB + AGM significant share |

Adoption is therefore a function of **vehicle age and origin** in a given market, not a global trend that arrives everywhere at once.

## What this means for a stockist

- Do not replace a vehicle's specified AGM with EFB (or vice versa) — the vehicle's charging strategy assumes the specified technology.
- Keep a mixed inventory: SLI for the older parc, EFB/AGM for newer start-stop vehicles.
- Confirm the technology against the vehicle, not the case size.

## Why a warmer test raises the number

Watch for this when comparing technologies: EFB and AGM ratings must be compared on the same CCA test basis and the same cycle-life definition, or the comparison is meaningless.
